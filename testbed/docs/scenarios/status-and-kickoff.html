Match lifecycle

When a match screen uses the query only, when it opens the subscription and when it stops. And why a stale status in the cache never makes anyone miss kickoff.

Three rules

The time decides when to startFrom kickoff − 15 min the client subscribes, whatever status the query returned.
The status decides when to stopOn the FULL_TIME frame the subgraph ends the stream and the client closes both channels.
The cache never goes past the windowA response with SCHEDULED expires by kickoff − 15 min; no cache serves “not started” after that.

What happens

    The SCHEDULED cache shrinks until the window

    Far from kickoff the response can stay cached for 5 min. In the last 5 min before the window, the max-age is the time left until it; under 1 s, no-store. In the window it goes back to 2 s, like live. Hover or drag a finger over the chart.

    Guarantee, tested in tests/lifecycle.test.mjs for any instant before the window: now + max-age ≤ kickoff − 15 min.

    What a stale status can cause

    The query returnedWhat may be wrongConsequenceRisk
    FULL_TIMENothing. It is the final state and does not go back.A long cache (1 h) is safe.none
    LIVE / HALF_TIMEThe match is already over.The client opens the subscription, the first frame brings FULL_TIME and the stream closes right after. Measured: under 1 s, without reconnecting.harmless
    SCHEDULED in the windowThe match has already started.None: the time says to subscribe, and the first frame brings the real status.none

    Limits

    • Match brought forward without rescheduling: if the match starts before kickoff − 15 min and nobody calls scheduleMatch, whoever is before the window only subscribes when it opens.
    • Rescheduling: scheduleMatch changes the kickoff in a new scoreboard version, so subscribers get the new time. Clients that only made the query may have the old one for up to 5 min; the catalog has its own cache.
    • Correction after the end (a goal overturned on review): FULL_TIME stays cached for up to 1 h, unless the cache is invalidated on that event.
    • Stats: the stats subgraph does not know the status, so its query keeps the 5 s cache. The client decides when to open and close the stats channel, based on the scoreboard.

    Where it lives

    • subgraphs/sports.mjs: matchPhase (phase by time and status) and scoreboardMaxAge (Cache-Control by status), used by the subgraph and the client.
    • subgraphs/app.mjs: the scoreboard ends the stream after the FULL_TIME frame; the scheduleMatch mutation.
    • client/lifecycle.mjs: watchMatch, a match screen (query, wait, subscription, end).
    • src/lib.rs (repository root): retention releases the entry at the end of the stream.
